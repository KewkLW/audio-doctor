Your audio pipeline, end to end

How a YouTube tab gets to your ears on this machine. Six layers, read top to bottom. Every box in the diagram is a real thing on your machine right now.

Arrows are the direction sound travels on playback. The microphone runs the same road upward: analog in → ADC → USB → ALSA capture → "RME In 1/2".

What each layer is, in plain words

1 · Apps

Make the sound

A browser tab, Discord, a game. Each one hands over a stream of numbers (samples) and never touches the hardware itself.

Musicians on stage.

2 · PipeWire

The mixing desk

One program that every app plugs into. It mixes all of them together, gives each its own volume fader, converts sample rates, and sends one combined signal to the device.

It's built as a graph: boxes (nodes) with sockets (ports) joined by wires (links). That's exactly what qpwgraph draws.

  • pipewire-pulse: a translator. The browser and Discord were written for an older server called PulseAudio. This lets them talk to PipeWire without knowing.
  • Sink = a place sound goes (output). Source = a place sound comes from (mic).
  • RME Phones 1/2 is a virtual sink I created: a stereo front door that forwards into channels 1/2 of the real 8-channel device.

The mixing desk and its patch bay.

2a · Three doors, one PipeWire

Pulse, JACK, native

Before PipeWire, Linux had two rival sound servers. PulseAudio was for desktop apps: easy, automatic, forgiving. JACK was for pro audio: tiny fixed buffers, every app on one shared clock, apps wired into each other like a patch bay.

PipeWire replaced both by speaking both languages. Apps still pick the door they were written for, but every door leads into the same mixing desk:

  • Pulse door: the browser, Discord, most native games, and Wine/Proton games (via winepulse).
  • JACK door: DAWs like Bitwig and Reaper, for low latency and per-channel ports. Your libjack is PipeWire's, from the pipewire-jack package. There is no separate JACK server.
  • Native door: pw-play, qpwgraph, and Ableton under Wine through PipeASIO.

Same desk, three kinds of input jacks.

2b · WirePlumber

The stage manager

It doesn't carry sound. It makes decisions: which device is the default, which profile a card uses (it's what keeps your RME on "Pro Audio"), and it wires every new app to the default output automatically. It also remembers per-app volumes.

The person who plugs cables in when a new musician walks on.

3 · ALSA + driver

The kernel's sound layer

ALSA (Advanced Linux Sound Architecture) is the part of Linux itself that knows about sound hardware. Every sound device becomes a numbered card: your RME is card 2, its playback device hw:2,0.

ALSA is deliberately dumb: one program at a time, fixed format (8 channels, 32-bit, 48 kHz). No mixing, no per-app volume. That's why PipeWire sits on top and holds it.

snd-usb-audio is the driver that turns those samples into USB packets. The RME is "class compliant", so this stock driver is the right one. RME makes no Linux driver.

The power amp's input jack: one cable in, no mixing.

4 · USB

The cable

A packet every 125 microseconds (8000 per second) carries the next slice of audio. The RME sends back timing feedback, so the PC sends exactly as fast as the RME's own clock runs ("async" mode). That clean clocking is a big reason RME gear sounds good.

A separate MIDI channel rides the same cable. That's how adi2 reads and changes the box's settings.

5 · ADI-2 Pro

A computer of its own

The box has its own routing (source: USB), a DSP that applies the volume knob, mute, EQ and crossfeed, then the DAC that turns numbers into voltage, and a headphone amp.

That's why "nothing coming out" can happen inside the box even when Linux is fine: it could be muted, turned down, or set to a different source. adi2 can see all of that.

6 · Ears

Analog from the jack on

After the jack it's plain electricity. Cable, plug seated, headphones.

The Bluetooth road is different

Your TMA-2 headphones skip layers 3–5 entirely: PipeWire → BlueZ (Linux Bluetooth) → radio → headphones. No ALSA card, no USB cable, and the audio is compressed (codec) before it leaves. That's why switching between RME and TMA-2 is just "which sink is default".

Glossary

Sample: one number = the speaker position at one instant.

Frame: one sample for every channel at the same instant.

Channel: one independent signal (left, right, AUX3…).

Node / port / link: PipeWire's box / socket / wire.

Sink / source: output / input.

Stream: an app's connection to PipeWire.

Profile: how a card's channels are presented ("Pro Audio" = raw numbered AUX channels).

Quantum: PipeWire's chunk size per cycle.

Class compliant: follows the USB audio standard, so no special driver needed.
